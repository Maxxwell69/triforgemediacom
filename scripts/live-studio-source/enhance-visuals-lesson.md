### TriForge Media Creator Onboarding — Module 3

## 1. Screen Adaptation — Controlling How Your Stream Fills the Screen

Most TikTok viewers watch in portrait on mobile, but creators broadcast in a mix of aspect ratios (16:9, 4:3, 9:16). Without adjustment, that mismatch either leaves black bars or crops the stream. Screen adaptation lets you decide how that tradeoff resolves for portrait-mode viewers (it has no effect on full-screen landscape viewing — that always shows the complete image).

**If you're live in landscape:**
- **Original** — shows the full image, but smaller on a portrait screen.
- **Crop** (4:3 or 1:1) — fills more of the screen, more visually striking, but trims the sides.
- **Recommendation:** crop for a bigger, more attention-grabbing display.

**If you're live in portrait:**
- **Fit to Screen** — no cropping, but may leave black bars top/bottom.
- **Fill Screen** — fills the full portrait frame, may crop left/right.
- **Recommendation:** fit to screen for gaming content, fill screen for non-gaming content.

**Dual layout:** your screen adaptation settings apply to both the landscape and portrait sides independently.

**Where to set it:** click **Screen adaptation** in the top-right corner of LIVE Studio (available whether you're going live through the app or a stream key — stream-key users configure it on the Go LIVE web page instead). Use mobile preview to check how it actually looks before going live.

## 2. Composing a Professional Scene Layout

A clean, deliberate layout reads as professional and keeps people watching longer. For a gaming stream, that means three elements working together: gameplay, facecam, and widgets.

**Gameplay:**
- Always capture the game directly — never point a camera at your physical screen.
- Portrait: scale gameplay to full screen width, with a minimum height of about 1/4 of the screen so detail stays readable.
- Landscape: go full-screen with the gameplay; if black bars show up, dress them with stickers or widgets rather than leaving them empty.

**Facecam overlay:**
- Showing your face drives real-time interaction and emotional connection — it's one of the highest-leverage layout decisions you can make.
- Portrait: give the facecam its own dedicated area, centered and full-size for the strongest impact. If you shrink or side-place it, decorate the empty space so it doesn't look unfinished.
- Landscape: tuck it into a corner of the gameplay at roughly 1/16–1/9 of total screen area — enough to see you clearly without blocking the action.

**Widgets:**
- Portrait: give widgets their own section; if layered onto gameplay or facecam, keep them small and sparse.
- Landscape: keep widgets limited and cornered so they never obstruct gameplay or your face.

Creators who want to serve both mobile and desktop audiences without picking one layout should combine this with dual layout (Module 3, Lesson 6).

## 3. Structuring Your Stream With Multiple Scenes

Splitting a stream into purpose-built scenes keeps content organized and viewers engaged. A typical gaming stream might use:

- **Intro scene** — full-screen camera, a welcome message (text source), and your game's logo, to hook people before gameplay starts.
- **Gameplay scene** — full-screen game capture with a facecam overlay and engagement widgets.
- **Chit-chat scene** — a chatbox widget, a camera source for direct conversation, and a text source pointing to your socials.

**Other useful scenes to build out:**
- **Break scene** — "Be right back" message, countdown timer, chatbox, and a looping clip so viewers stay entertained during a pause.
- **Announcement scene** — a large, clear textbox for important updates.
- **Special event scene** — themed background, graphics, and celebratory elements for milestones.

Pair this with transition effects (next lesson) so switching between them feels intentional, not jarring.

## 4. Transition Effects Between Scenes

Smooth scene switches read as professional; hard cuts read as sloppy. Set them up under **Settings > Scene transition**, pick an effect from the dropdown, and adjust its options. Note: transitions only work between scenes sharing the same aspect ratio.

**Source move — the standout transition effect:**
Source move identifies matching sources across two scenes and animates their movement/resizing between them — for example, smoothly shifting focus from your gameplay window to your camera view instead of hard-cutting.

Setup:
1. Select **Source move** as your effect.
2. Set the transition duration.
3. Choose one or more **source matching rules** — how LIVE Studio decides which sources in the two scenes are "the same":
   - **Copied camera** — matches an original camera source to its copy across scenes.
   - **Name** — matches sources sharing the same name.
   - **Type** — matches sources of the same type (e.g., two image sources).
   - If you select multiple rules, priority runs Copied camera → Name → Type; ties go to whichever source was created first.
4. Set an entry/exit animation direction for any unmatched sources (they'll move in or out during the transition).

## 5. Dynamic Effects — One-Click Cinematic Moments

Dynamic effects bundle layout changes, video filters, sound effects, and camera effects into a single trigger — built for highlighting big reactions or funny moments without fumbling through menus mid-stream. Find the panel in the control bar at the bottom of LIVE Studio.

**Five built-in presets:**
- **Camera focus** — puts your camera front and center. Good for chatting between rounds or reacting to a big moment or gift.
- **Game focus** — puts your game capture front and center. Good for hyping a team fight or a boss battle.
- **That hurt!** — black-and-white filter plus a sad sound effect, for character deaths or failed challenges.
- **Wait, what?** — zooms your camera in with a quirky sound effect, for weird bugs or confusing moments.
- **Big win!** — zooms your camera in with a celebratory sound effect, for big victories.

**Building a custom effect:**
1. Open the dynamic effects panel and click **Add new**.
2. Name it.
3. Stack in any combination of: focus switch (camera or main content), video filter, sound effect, camera effect.
4. Set the duration.
5. Save. (Right-click any effect in the panel to edit or delete it later.)

**Triggering effects hands-free:** beyond clicking or hotkeys, effects can fire automatically off:
- **Game highlights** (e.g., a victory triggers a "Big win!" effect automatically).
- **Keyword voice commands** (e.g., saying "I'm dead" fires a preset effect).
- **Gestures** (e.g., a thumbs-up in frame fires a preset effect).

Set these under the effect's **Triggers > Select trigger**, then **Apply**. Heads up: voice and gesture recognition use CPU/memory — if performance is tight, switch to manual triggering or disable those recognition features.

**Game highlight auto-trigger** currently supports 11 titles: Mobile Legends: Bang Bang, League of Legends, Valorant, CS:GO, Counter-Strike, Fortnite, PUBG Mobile, PUBG: BATTLEGROUNDS, Call of Duty (Battle Royale), Call of Duty: Mobile (Battle Royale), and Garena Free Fire (Battle Royale). Turn it on from the dynamic effects panel — once enabled it runs without adding system load.

**Before using dynamic effects:** make sure your scene already has both a camera source and a main content source (game/window capture) visible — the effects need both to work correctly.

**Pro tips:**
- Assign hotkeys to your go-to effects for instant access without opening the LIVE Studio window — a Stream Deck makes this even smoother.
- Every creator's style is different — experiment to find what actually lands with your audience.

## 6. Dual Layout — Streaming Portrait and Landscape at Once

Dual layout = portrait + landscape, running simultaneously, so viewers automatically get whichever orientation fits their device.

**Why it's worth setting up:**
- **Better viewing experience** — adapts automatically, no cropping or distortion regardless of device.
- **Higher engagement and retention** — letting viewers pick their preferred layout increases satisfaction and the odds they come back.
- **Wider reach** — appeals to PC, console, and mobile viewers all at once.

**How the sync actually works:**
- You build the **portrait layout first** — since most TikTok discovery happens in portrait, this is your priority and determines what gets pulled into landscape.
- Any source/widget added in portrait automatically appears in landscape too. You can then resize/reposition those items in landscape, but their appearance and settings always stay consistent with the portrait version.
- Once viewers are hooked by your portrait content, many will switch to landscape/full-screen for a more immersive view — so keep landscape focused on your core content (like game capture) with minimal distractions to maximize watch time.

**Why sync instead of letting the two layouts differ freely?** Early on, some creators used separate orientations to show entirely different (and sometimes non-compliant) content hidden behind a compliant one. Syncing prevents that kind of misuse.

**Switching a scene to dual layout:** right-click the scene, choose **Switch layout**, select **Dual layout**.

**How content adapts when you switch:**
- **Full-screen video sources** — scaled proportionally to fit and centered, never cropped or distorted (landscape→portrait: scaled to fit width; portrait→landscape: scaled to fit height).
- **Non-full-screen video sources** — repositioned automatically based on relative placement.
- **Full-screen image sources** (typically backgrounds) — scaled to fill the new view while preserving aspect ratio.
- **Widgets** — rearranged per the current layout template. Note: chatbox and ranking widgets are dropped when switching landscape → dual layout, since they aren't supported in portrait.

## 8. Dialing In LIVE Quality

Higher resolution and bitrate look better but eat more network and system resources — push too far and you get buffering and dropped frames even on a decent connection. The goal is balance, not maximum settings.

**Auto settings:** LIVE Studio runs a network/hardware test during initial setup and suggests parameters. Click **Run** anytime to re-test as your setup or connection changes, then choose **Quality priority** (sharper image) or **Performance priority** (smoother playback) based on the results.

**Quality presets:**
- **1080P60 / 720P60** — high-definition, high frame rate; best for gaming streams.
- **1080P / 720P** — standard clarity, moderate frame rate; good for chat, performance content, or anything using camera effects.
- **480P** — lower quality, lighter on bandwidth; best on slower connections.

**Manually tuning each parameter:**
- **Resolution** — 1080P for max clarity on strong hardware; 720P is the safe default for most creators since most viewers watch on mobile anyway; 480P for constrained networks/hardware.
- **FPS** — 45/60 for gaming and fast action; 30 for most other content, especially with camera effects running; 20/25 as the floor for lifestyle content.
- **Video bitrate** — leave on default; LIVE Studio balances it against your other settings automatically. A lower bitrate doesn't automatically mean worse quality or dropped frames.
- **Audio bitrate** — leave on default for the same reason; higher generally means better audio quality.
- **Encoder** — hardware encoders are recommended since they offload work to your GPU instead of your CPU. LIVE Studio can auto-select the right one for your machine.
- **Stream latency** — low latency favors real-time viewer interaction; higher latency trades responsiveness for stability, useful on weaker connections.
- **Codec compatibility mode** — only touch this if you're seeing visual corruption (like a green screen instead of your actual video) caused by an encoder/platform mismatch. Leave on default otherwise.
