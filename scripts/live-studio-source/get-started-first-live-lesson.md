## 1. The LIVE Studio Interface, In Plain Terms

Before touching any settings, new creators need a mental map of the app. LIVE Studio is built around a few core building blocks — scenes, sources, widgets, and audio devices — and everything else is just configuration on top of those.

> ![LIVE Studio main interface overview](https://p16-ttak-va.tiktokcdn.com/tos-maliva-i-m3rm1y8daf-us/88c71801e6e14e7e128edfd593e92b18.png~tplv-m3rm1y8daf-default:0:0:q75.image)

Here's what each part of the interface does for you:

**Scenes & sources panel** — Add scenes to build different "looks" for your stream and switch between them live. Add up to 20 sources per scene. Toggle between dual, landscape, and portrait layouts depending on where your viewers are watching from.

**Scene canvas** — Drag, resize, and reposition every source exactly how you want viewers to see it — what's on the canvas is what goes out live. Use mobile preview to check how it reads on the actual TikTok app, and hit record to save your best moments. Set your title, description, and cover art here too — this is what pulls in viewers browsing the app.

**Metrics panel** — Track unique viewers, new followers, and likes in real time, and keep an eye on Creator Camp challenges (reward challenges, gaming level challenges) while you're live.

**LIVE chat** — See every comment as it comes in, plus real-time alerts for gifts, joins, likes, new followers, and new subscribers.

**Audio mixer** — Add and configure every audio input/output plus any media you're playing, control volume per device, and layer on audio effects.

**System monitor** — Keep an eye on CPU, memory, network, frame drops, and frame rate while you're live so a performance issue doesn't blindside you mid-stream.

Once a creator has this map in their head, every other lesson in this library is just "which button in which panel."

## 2. Understanding Scenes

A scene is the canvas your viewers actually see — everything you layer into it (camera, screen share, text, images, web pages) shows up live exactly as arranged.

**Layout types:**
- **Portrait** — built for mobile viewers watching vertically.
- **Landscape** — best for widescreen content like PC gaming.
- **Dual layout** — lets viewers choose portrait or landscape, covering both audiences at once.

**Adding a scene:** pick a layout type, click **Add scene**, choose a template, and click **Add**. You can keep customizing after it's created.

**Customizing a scene:** drag sources directly on the canvas for quick edits, or click **Edit** on the scene for the full editing view — which also lets you preview exactly how the scene will look in both the **LIVE** and **For You** feed tabs on TikTok, so you're designing for the real user experience, not guessing.

## 3. Understanding Sources

A source is anything you drop into a scene — camera feed, screen capture, text, image, whatever adds substance to what viewers see. Add one by clicking **Add source** under your selected scene and picking a type.

**The source types, and when to use each:**

- **Game / Display / Window capture** — the three flavors of screen sharing. Game capture grabs the specific game at high resolution (built for dedicated gaming streams, though not every game supports it). Display capture shares your whole desktop — great for tutorials that bounce between apps. Window capture shares just one application window — ideal for a focused software walkthrough. Full setup steps live in the capture-source lesson below.
- **Camera** — puts your face on screen, which is the single biggest lever for trust and engagement. Full setup in the camera-source lesson below.
- **Capture card** — pulls audio/video from a gaming console over USB/HDMI. Covered in its own lesson below.
- **Cast** — mirrors your phone screen over Wi-Fi or cable. Covered in its own lesson below.
- **Text** — drops custom text on screen for announcements, titles, or alerts.
- **Video** — adds a pre-recorded local video into your scene.
- **Image** — adds a local image for backgrounds, branding, or visual aids.
- **Link** — embeds a live webpage preview into your scene.

## 4. Setting Up a Camera Source

Showing your face is one of the fastest ways to build trust and hold viewers. Here's the setup:

1. Plug your camera into your computer.
2. In your active scene, click **Add source > Camera**.
3. Dial in the settings:
   - **Adjustment** — rotate/flip the feed as needed.
   - **Camera** — pick from any connected cameras.
   - **Resolution** — match or stay below your LIVE quality setting for best performance.
   - **FPS** — 60 for the smoothest video, 30 if you're running heavier camera effects. Either way, stay at or below your LIVE quality setting.
   - **Video format** — YUY2 gives the cleanest color at the cost of bandwidth (good for stronger setups); NV12/I420 trades a bit of quality for efficiency on tighter bandwidth.
   - **Exposure, saturation, contrast, sharpness, hue, white balance, color range, color space** — fine-tune the actual look of your video image.
   - **Background** — remove/replace your background with a virtual one.
4. Layer on **Enhance**, **Effects**, and **Filters** to polish the look further.

**Camera-shy creators still have options:** digital masks add playfulness and a layer of privacy, animated avatars mirror your expressions without showing your real face, and camera effects can change your look enough to feel more comfortable on screen.

**Quick wins for a better on-camera look:**
- Light with a softbox or ring light for even, flattering coverage.
- Set the camera at eye level.
- Keep the background clean or use a virtual one.
- Actually look into the camera and react to chat — it reads as real interaction, not a broadcast.

## 5. Sharing Your Computer Screen

Three capture types cover every screen-sharing scenario:

**Game capture** — the most efficient way to stream a PC game.
1. Open the game.
2. **Add source > Game capture**.
3. Pick a mode: **Capture any full-screen game** (auto-detects whatever's running full-screen) or **Capture selected game** (locks to one game — keep that window open).
4. Extra options: **Higher compatibility** switches to memory capture if shared-texture capture isn't working (less efficient, so only use when needed); **Capture cursor** toggles whether your mouse pointer is visible to viewers.
   - Note: not every game supports game capture — fall back to display or window capture if you hit issues.

**Display capture** — shares your entire desktop, ideal for tutorials or multi-app workflows.
1. **Add source > Display capture**.
2. Pick a display if you have more than one.
3. Toggle **Capture cursor** as needed.

**Window capture** — shares one specific application window.
1. **Add source > Window capture**.
2. Select the window (keep it open during the stream).
3. Toggle **Capture cursor** as needed.

## 6. Casting Your Phone Screen

LIVE Studio can mirror a phone screen, which beats streaming directly from the phone once you want more than the bare-bones mobile app experience:

- **Richer content** — layer in cameras, text, alerts, goals, and viewer-ranking widgets around the phone feed.
- **Bigger workspace** — manage the whole stream from a proper desktop interface.
- **Full LIVE tools** — co-host, Play Together, Gift vote, LIVE goals.
- **Real-time metrics** — adjust on the fly based on actual performance data.

**iOS:** **Add source > Cast (iOS)**, connect via cable or wireless Screen Mirroring, follow the on-screen prompts, then **Add source**.

**Android:** **Add source > Cast (Android)**, connect via cable or wireless through the TikTok app, follow the prompts, then **Add source**.

**Keep it smooth:**
- Use a stable Wi-Fi connection or go wired to avoid lag.
- Turn on Do Not Disturb — viewers see the *entire* phone screen, notifications included.
- Lock screen orientation so it doesn't rotate mid-stream.
- Keep the phone charging throughout.

## 7. Adding a Console via Capture Card

For console gaming, a capture card pulls in the raw HDMI video/audio for noticeably better quality than a phone recording a TV.

1. Plug the console's HDMI output into the capture card, then connect the card to your computer via USB or PCIe.
2. **Add source > Capture card**.
3. **Video setup:** pick the capture card as your video device, set resolution and FPS for a smooth clear image, match the video format to the card's capabilities, and set color range (usually **All**) and color space (typically **709** for standard HD).
4. **Audio setup:** select the audio signal from the card (or **Don't capture** to skip it), configure bit width/sample rate, and choose an audio output mode — **Capture audio only** (viewers hear it, you don't through this path), **Output audio (DirectSound)**, or **Output audio (WaveOut)** (both route it to you and viewers).

## 8. Connecting a Microphone

Clean audio matters as much as clean video, especially for talk-heavy content.

LIVE Studio auto-detects your computer's built-in mic on first setup — fine for a quick start, but not your best sound.

**Adding an external mic:**
1. Plug it in.
2. Open the **audio mixer** from the control bar.
3. Under **Microphones**, click **Add**, select your device, configure settings, and click **Add**.

**Dialing in mic settings:**
- **Volume** — loud enough to hear clearly, not so hot it clips.
- **Noise suppression** — cuts background noise.
- **Convert to mono** — flattens a multi-channel mic to mono when needed.
- **Audio offset** — fixes any lag between your voice and the video.
- **Audio monitoring** — put on headphones to hear exactly what viewers hear, live.

**Mute everything fast:** click the mic icon in the control bar to mute/unmute all connected microphones at once — useful for stepping away or handling an off-stream interruption.

## 9. Previewing Video & Audio Before You Go Live

Last check before hitting go — confirm everything actually looks and sounds right.

**Video preview:** click the mobile preview button above the scene preview to see it from a viewer's perspective.
- Landscape scenes preview in portrait (the default first impression for most viewers) or full landscape.
- Portrait scenes preview in general or gaming view, depending on content type.
- Dual-layout scenes don't support mobile preview yet — use the scene editing view instead (see Module 2, Lesson 2: Understanding Scenes).

**Audio test:**
1. Open the audio mixer.
2. Click the mic's settings, go to **General settings**, and switch on **Audio monitoring**. Save.
3. Click **Test audio** in the mixer to hear your own voice through your connected speaker or headset.
4. Click **Testing...** when you're done to end the test.
