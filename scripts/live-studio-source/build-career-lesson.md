# Lesson: Build a Career
### TriForge Media Creator Onboarding — Module 8

Everything up to this point is mechanics. This module is strategy: how to turn a stream into a sustainable creator career through goal-setting, content strategy, personal branding, and building a Fan Club that actually deepens loyalty over time.

---

## 1. Optimizing Your LIVE Content Strategy

**Start with your goal.** Get clear on why you're actually doing this — building an audience, chasing income, developing a craft, whatever it is — because that goal should shape where you spend your time. Different aspirations demand different effort.

**Theme every stream.** Having a plan for a session beats just "playing games." What makes your stream worth watching over just playing the game yourself? Going somewhere new? Taking on a tough boss? Predetermined talking points drive better engagement, and knowing your session's specific angle makes the content easier to promote and repurpose later. Even spontaneous highlight moments should be noted for reuse.

**Reuse your content.** Don't let a stream live and die once — cut it into highlight videos and post them to TikTok to extend its life and pull in new viewers.

**Stay consistent.** Followers want predictability. Keep a visible, steady streaming schedule on your profile and during streams. Recurring formats — Supporter Sundays, a First-Time Play series, monthly game reviews — make regular posting easier and give followers something to anticipate.

**Choosing what to stream long-term:**
- Popular games bring more viewers but more competition; niche games bring less of both. Pick based on your actual situation, not the trend.
- Find your own lane and stick with it — technique-focused, comedic, distinctive voice, whatever fits — and build your reputation around it.
- Keep things varied: team up with friends, bring viewers in as gaming partners, occasionally break format with a different game, or react to content together with your audience.

**Building your brand.** Work through these questions deliberately:
- **What's your persona?** Funny? Approachable? Cause-driven? A specialist in a particular game or strategy?
- **What do you want to be known for?** Tournaments? Charity work? A specific game niche? Interviews and collabs? Comedy or speedruns?
- **What do you actually talk about, and want to?** Some creators use their platform as pure escapism, others use it for advocacy — pick what's genuinely you, and don't be afraid to talk about things outside gaming.
- **What content do you make, and how do you build community around it?** Your stream is likely the hub, but think about your presence across other platforms too.
- **Who are you trying to reach?** Getting specific about your target audience opens up more content opportunities, not fewer.

Once those are answered, you have real groundwork for typography, color palette, and overall style.

**Brand & persona, why it matters:**
- **Identity** — easy to recognize, memorable.
- **Reputation** — how would your top fans actually describe you?
- Concrete brand elements to define: username/persona/profile picture, tone of voice in written posts, outfits and scripts, catchphrases/slogans, color palette/logo/emotes, Discord name/sub badge name.

**Defining your brand — a quick exercise:**
1. Pick one word that captures it: charming, rebellious, innovative, goofy, etc.
2. Find and write down synonyms of that word.
3. Turn it into one sentence describing your motivations and goals.

**Retaining viewer loyalty comes down to trust and authenticity:**
- Rewatch your own streams and take notes on what's working.
- Refresh your profile picture, Stream Deck setup, lighting, and backdrop periodically.
- Invest in an editor to experiment with new video styles, overlays, or emotes.
- Appeal to viewers' emotions — genuine connection outperforms polish alone.

## 2. Building a Fan Club

A Fan Club is your core community structure — think of it like managing a clan or guild that deepens over time, not a one-off perk.

**How it works:**
- Viewers join by sending a 1-coin **Heart Me** Gift.
- Fans earn points by completing daily missions — Heart Me, Popular Votes, sending Gifts, watching, and commenting.
- Points level fans up and unlock privileges.
- Every fan point also counts as a **popular point** for your Fan Club overall (1:1) — the top 99 creators by popular points get featured on the Popular LIVE ranking, a real visibility boost.

**Why it's worth building deliberately:**
- **Traffic** — a Heart Me join also follows you, and Fan Club members tend to stay longer, watch more, and chat more, which makes your stream read as more active to new arrivals.
- **Revenue** — the 1-coin entry point is a low-friction "icebreaker" for casual viewers; ongoing engagement drives return visits and consistent light support (Heart Me, Popular Vote, badges); and over time fans progress through loyalty tiers, unlocking higher-value participation, just like leveling in a guild.

**Five ways to actually get new viewers to join:**

1. **Make it visible** — use Fan Club/Heart-Me stickers, and pin fan comments explaining how to join ("Tap Heart-Me!"). Having fans explain it themselves reads as more natural than the creator directly asking.
2. **Show exactly where to click** — many viewers genuinely don't know where the button is. Point to the Heart Me button (top-left on mobile) directly: "Heart Me button is right here, that gets you in the Fan Club."
3. **Explain the actual benefit** — one short line connecting identity + perks + community works better than a bare ask: "Join the Fan Club to level up," "Join my Fan Club to get TTS," "Tap Heart Me to join my team."
4. **Use in-LIVE goals tied to Heart-Me** — set a target (50, 100, "x2," next badge level), show a real-time progress bar, and narrate milestones ("5 more," "halfway," "push for x2"). This gamifies giving and frames it as a team objective rather than a request.
5. **Promote with short videos** — posted ahead of a LIVE, these build awareness and curiosity before viewers even arrive, increasing the odds they join once they do.

**Fan Club entry points — for you as the creator:**
- **Before going live:** LIVE Center → the **Team** button → view all Creator's Team member info.
- **After a LIVE:** the LIVE Summary page → **Team** section → opens the Fan Club panel.
- **Anytime:** the Fan Club 🧡 icon at the end of your Profile Frame.

**Entry points for viewers:**
- New fans: tap the small heart icon behind your profile frame → prompted to send a Heart Me and join.
- Existing fans: same icon opens the Fan Club panel showing their level progress and daily missions.
- Alternatively: the **Exclusive** tab of the Gift panel, selecting the Heart Me gift.

**The Fan Club panel — creator's view includes:** your Popular LIVE ranking, unread group chat messages, overall Fan Club performance insights, a **Members** tab (filterable by Top Members or 24-hour Contributors) showing level badges, achievements, and daily mission completion, a **Fan Club Zone** tab for customizing your club's name and gift photo, and **Fan Club Activities** like the Fantastic Journey event and Club Quest progress.

**The Fan Club panel — viewer's view:** shows level progress and daily missions, a "Your Rewards" journey view of what unlocks at each level (levels run 1–50, with every privilege unlocked at 50), and level-up progress toward badges, gifts, and special effects.

**FAQ:**
- **Member cap?** None — no limit on Fan Club size.
- **Can a viewer join multiple Fan Clubs?** Yes, with independent level progress per creator.
- **Highest level?** 50 — remind members they still need to stay active past 50 to avoid going dormant.
- **What does a greyed-out Fan Club badge mean?** The fan didn't claim points for 7 consecutive LIVE days and went dormant — their unlocked perks are frozen until they send another Heart Me to reactivate.
- **Does reactivating lose progress?** No — frozen level and privileges are simply unfrozen again.
- **Can fans leave?** Yes, via Profile > Member badge > Member rewards > Manage > Leave — but leaving forfeits all level progress and privileges permanently; rejoining starts from zero.
